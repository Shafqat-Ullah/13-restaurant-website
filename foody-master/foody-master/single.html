How To Cook Pasta?

A simple guide to cooking pasta the Italian way

There is nothing complicated about good pasta, but small details make all the difference. Follow our chef's tips and you will never boil a dull bowl again.

Start with the pot: use plenty of water, roughly one litre for every 100 grams of pasta, and season it with about ten grams of salt per litre. The water should taste of the sea.

Our rule of thumb: cook the pasta until it is just shy of al dente, then finish it in the sauce with a splash of starchy water. It is the starch that makes everything cling and sing.

Save a cup of the cooking water before draining. It is liquid gold, ready to loosen a sauce, emulsify a dressing or rescue a dish that has gone a little dry.

Matching pasta to sauce

Long, smooth shapes such as spaghetti pair best with silky, oil-based sauces, while ridged tubes like rigatoni trap chunky ragus and creamy sauces beautifully.

When you toss pasta with sauce in the pan, use a gentle folding motion for a minute or two. This is how the sauce really gets inside every curve and fold.

Finish with good extra-virgin olive oil, a shower of parmesan and a grind of black pepper. Serve immediately, in warmed bowls, and do not let it wait.

Experiment with fresh herbs, chilli flakes or a squeeze of lemon to make each plate your own. Cooking should be fun, not prescriptive.

Come by the restaurant on a Monday, when we teach our pasta masterclass, or simply order the cacio e pepe and taste what technique really means.

Buon appetito from all of us at Foody, and remember: the best pasta is the pasta you make with a smile.
